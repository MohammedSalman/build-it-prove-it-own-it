# Interview guide (homework: two real interviews before Week 3)

About 20 minutes per interview, plus 10 minutes to write up. Interview people who could
really use your festival: students, a society organiser, someone who volunteers at events.
Not your teammates.

## Before

- **Goal.** Write one sentence: what do you need to learn? Example: *"How do students deal
  with events that are full, and what happens to places that free up?"*
- **Consent.** Say who you are, what the notes are for (a university project), and that
  they can stop at any time. Ask before recording anything. Don't write down their full name;
  use a label such as "P1, second-year, games society".
- **Don't pitch.** Don't describe your app or your idea until the end, if at all.

## Questions: ask about the past, not opinions of your idea

Start broad, then follow the facts.

1. "Tell me about the last event you really wanted to go to." (What, when, how did you hear?)
2. "How did you get a place?" / "What happened when it was full?"
3. "What did you do next?" Keep asking this. Facts come from the follow-ups.
4. "Has a place ever come free after you'd given up? How did you find out?"
5. "Last time something sold out fast, who got in? How did people feel about it?"
6. "What did you try that didn't work?"
7. "Is there anything I should have asked?"

Good follow-ups: "When was that?", "How many times?", "Who else was involved?", "Can you
show me?" (the chat, the email, the booking page).

## Avoid

| Instead of… | Ask… |
|---|---|
| "Would you use a waitlist?" (hypothetical) | "What did you do the last time an event was full?" |
| "Wouldn't it be great to get a text when a place frees up?" (leading) | "How did you find out about the last change to an event?" |
| "Is first come, first served fair?" (opinion) | "Last time tickets went fast, who got them? What did people say afterwards?" |
| "How much would you pay for…?" (future) | "What did you last pay for, and how?" |
